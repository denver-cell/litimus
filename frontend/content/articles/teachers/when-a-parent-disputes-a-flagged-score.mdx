## Lead with the process, not the defense

A parent hearing “the AI detector flagged my kid” for the first time is hearing it as an accusation with a machine standing behind it. Walking through the actual process — policy, your own reading, a baseline comparison, then a conversation with the student — reframes it as a procedure, not a verdict already reached.

## Bring the same evidence you would use with the student

Baseline comparisons, the actual reasons behind the score, and what the conversation with the student covered. A parent asking “how do you know” deserves the same specific answer a student would, not a restated score.

## Do not let the meeting become a re-litigation without the student present

If the student already had their conversation and the matter was resolved or is still open, involve them in the follow-up rather than settling it entirely between adults. The process depends on hearing from the person who actually wrote the work.

## Set expectations before it happens

A policy that is visible to parents too — on a syllabus, a portal, an assignment sheet — means the first time a parent hears about your AI-detection process is not the day their child is flagged.
