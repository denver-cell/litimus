## Say the rule for this format specifically

A general course policy on AI assistance does not automatically answer whether a take-home exam is treated like an essay or like a closed-book test. State it on the exam itself, not just in the syllabus.

## A baseline still matters, even under time pressure

Compare a take-home answer against the same student’s in-class writing or earlier timed work, not just against the class average. Writing produced quickly under a deadline naturally has different rhythm than a polished essay — that alone is not evidence of anything.

## Consider what a shorter window actually reduces

A tighter deadline reduces how much revision and drafting is realistic, which can make a heavily-generated answer more likely to still read as generated rather than smoothed over. That cuts both ways: it can also make honest writing read as rushed and uneven, which is its own kind of ordinary.

## If you scan, scan before grading starts

Running checks after grades are already informally decided invites the score to confirm a hunch rather than inform a reading. Build the scan into the same step as your first read, per <a href="/for/teachers">the five-step process</a>.
