## Two different questions

A similarity or plagiarism check asks whether text matches something that already exists somewhere else. An AI-detection score asks whether text reads as statistically typical of machine-generated writing. A passage can score high on one and low on the other — they are not measuring the same thing, and a high AI score is not evidence of copying.

## Why the confusion is common

Platforms that offer both checks in one report make it easy to read two separate numbers as one combined verdict. Turnitin explicitly separates its similarity report from its AI writing report for this reason — treat them the same way even when a tool doesn’t force you to.

## What each one actually supports

A similarity match gives you a specific source to compare against — you can look at both texts side by side. An AI-detection score gives you a pattern, not a source. Do not treat the second as if it had the specificity of the first.

## In practice

If a report shows both, read them separately, and follow <a href="/for/teachers">the five-step process</a> for the AI-detection portion specifically. A plagiarism match calls for a different, more direct conversation entirely.
