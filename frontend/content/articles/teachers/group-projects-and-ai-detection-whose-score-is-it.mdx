## Scan sections, not just the whole document

If your platform allows it, scan each student’s contributed section separately rather than the merged whole — version history in Google Docs can usually attribute paragraphs to an author even after merging. A single high score on a combined document tells you nothing about which student’s writing produced it.

## Don’t assume the score applies evenly

One student is at least as likely to have written the flagged section as any other, and a score on 3,000 combined words says little about any single 500-word contribution. Read section by section before you form a view.

## Ask the group together, then individually

A group conversation surfaces who wrote what. If one student’s account doesn’t match what the version history shows, a private follow-up with just that student is fairer to the rest of the group than raising it in front of everyone.

## The policy question worth settling upfront

Decide, and tell students before the project starts, whether AI-assistance rules apply per section or to the group as a whole — see <a href="/for/teachers/setting-an-ai-assistance-policy-students-will-read">setting a policy students will actually read</a>. Groups negotiate this poorly themselves if the rule was never stated.
