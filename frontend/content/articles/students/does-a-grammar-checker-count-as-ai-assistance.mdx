## The useful distinction

A tool that corrects what you already wrote — spelling, grammar, a suggested synonym — is editing. A tool that produces sentences you didn’t write from a prompt is generation. Most policies mean to ban or require disclosure of the second, not the first, but few say so explicitly.

## Where it gets genuinely unclear

A more aggressive suggestion — “rephrase this sentence” — sits closer to the line than a red squiggly underline. If a tool rewrote a whole sentence for you and you accepted it without changing it further, that is arguably closer to generation than correction, whatever the tool is branded as.

## What to do if the policy doesn’t say

Ask before you submit, not after a score comes back mixed. Naming the specific tool and what you used it for turns a vague worry into a concrete question your instructor can actually answer. It matches the same habit that helps with disclosure generally — see <a href="/for/students">checking your own writing</a> for the broader approach.

## What it doesn’t change

Whatever the answer, keeping your draft history is still the strongest thing you can point to if a score comes back higher than expected.
