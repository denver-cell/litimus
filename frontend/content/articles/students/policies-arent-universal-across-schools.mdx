## Why the assumption is so easy to make

Once you have learned one instructor’s or one school’s rule, it feels like general knowledge rather than a specific policy. Transferring schools, switching instructors, or even moving between two classes in the same term can each carry a different rule underneath a similar-looking assignment.

## Check per class, not per habit

Read the actual policy for each course rather than defaulting to what worked before, even within the same semester. A habit that was fine in one class can be exactly the thing another class’s policy prohibits.

## If the policy genuinely isn’t stated

Ask, rather than assume the most permissive reading. An instructor who has not written a policy down usually still has an expectation, and asking directly avoids a guess going the wrong way in either direction.

## This applies to disclosure requirements too

Some schools require disclosure of any AI use; others only restrict specific uses without requiring it be stated. See <a href="/for/students/citing-ai-assistance-without-losing-marks">how to disclose clearly</a> once you know which kind of policy you are actually working under.
