## Why this format draws more attention

Without a supervised room, an instructor has less built-in assurance that you worked alone, which is part of why take-home exams get checked more often than a similar essay assignment. That is about the format, not about you specifically.

## Keep the same draft trail, on a tighter clock

Writing in a document with version history, even under exam time pressure, costs you nothing and is the strongest thing you can point to afterward. A frantic, uneven writing history is still more convincing than no history at all.

## Rushed writing looks different from generated writing, and that’s fine

Short deadlines naturally produce less polished, more uneven prose. That is a normal feature of writing under time pressure, not evidence of anything — but it is worth knowing that an instructor reading a mid-range score on a rushed answer should weigh the deadline, and it is fair to say so if asked.

## Read the exam’s specific rule, not just the course policy

A take-home exam may have its own AI-assistance rule separate from the course’s general policy. Confirm it before you start, not after you have already used a tool the general policy would have allowed elsewhere.
