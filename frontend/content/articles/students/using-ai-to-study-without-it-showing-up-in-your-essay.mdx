## The useful separation

Asking a tool to explain a concept, summarize a source, or quiz you is research — the same category as reading a textbook or asking a tutor. Asking a tool to produce sentences you then submit is generation. A detector, and most policies, care about the second, not the first.

## Close the tool before you start writing

If you studied with AI help and then write the essay from what you now understand, without the tool open in another tab to lean on for phrasing, the writing is genuinely yours in the way that matters. That is the simplest test to apply to yourself.

## Notice if a phrase feels borrowed, not just understood

If a sentence you write feels like it came from the tool’s explanation rather than from your own understanding of it, that is worth rewriting in your own words — not because it is against most rules, but because it is a sign you understood the phrasing more than the idea.

## Disclose the study use if your policy asks for it

Some policies ask for disclosure of any AI use, including for studying, not just for drafting. Read what your specific policy requires — see <a href="/for/students/does-a-grammar-checker-count-as-ai-assistance">where the disclosure line tends to sit</a> when it is not stated explicitly.
