## Bring evidence, not just disagreement

Version history from Google Docs or Word, your notes and outline, and — if you ran one yourself beforehand — your own Litimus report from before submission. See <a href="/for/students/keeping-proof-your-essay-is-yours">keeping proof your essay is yours</a> for what is worth saving as a habit, not just after the fact.

## Name what you think happened

If you write in a second language, used a template your course provided, or tend toward short, even sentences, say so specifically rather than just asserting the score is wrong. A specific, plausible explanation is easier for an instructor to weigh than a general objection.

## Ask to see the reasons, not just the number

A score alone is hard to argue with. The readings behind it — which phrases, which pattern — are something you can actually respond to point by point.

## If the process feels unfair

Most institutions have an academic integrity appeals process separate from any individual instructor’s judgment. <a href="/guides/falsely-accused-of-ai-writing">This guide</a> covers what that looks like in more detail than fits here.
