## The overlap isn’t a coincidence

A detector measures sentence-length variation, vocabulary spread, and departure from formulaic phrasing because those are statistically rare in generated text. They are also, separately, what admissions and scholarship readers have always said makes a personal statement stand out. The advice was already good before any detector existed.

## The genre pulls toward sameness on its own

Personal statements share a small set of familiar shapes — the turning point, the lesson learned, the reflective close — because they work, which is exactly why so many essays reach for them. That pull toward familiar structure is worth resisting for the reader’s sake as much as for any score.

## One concrete detail does more than a polished paragraph

A specific image, a specific line of dialogue, a specific object — these are hard for either a reader or a detector to mistake for generic writing, and they are usually the parts of an essay people remember.

## Use a mid-range score as an edit note, not a verdict

If your own check comes back higher than you expected, look at which paragraphs it flagged and ask whether they are the more generic sections of the essay. Often they are, and that is useful to know regardless of the number attached to it.
