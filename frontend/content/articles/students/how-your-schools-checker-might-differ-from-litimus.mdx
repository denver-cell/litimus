## Different tools, different thresholds

What counts as “likely AI” on one platform might sit in another’s mixed range entirely. If you check your own writing with Litimus before submitting to a different institutional tool, do not expect the two numbers to match exactly — read both for their reasons, not just their scores.

## Your school’s tool is the one that counts for your grade

Whatever you learn from checking your own work elsewhere, the platform your institution actually uses is the one whose result matters for any formal process. Use your own check as preparation, not as a substitute for knowing what your school’s tool is likely to say.

## Ask what your institution’s tool actually reports

Some platforms show only a single flag, not a score or reasons. If that is what your school uses, you will not get the kind of detail Litimus gives you — which makes checking your own writing beforehand, where you can see the reasoning, more useful, not less.

## The underlying limitation is the same everywhere

Every detector, including Litimus, gives a probability built from writing patterns, not a certainty. That caveat applies just as much to whatever tool your school runs as it does here — see <a href="/guides/how-ai-detectors-work">how AI detectors work</a> for why that is true of the approach generally, not any one product.
