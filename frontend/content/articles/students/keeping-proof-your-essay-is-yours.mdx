<Callout>

**What this is for.** Keeping evidence of honest work — not a way around your school’s rules. If your course bans AI assistance or requires disclosure, that still applies regardless of what any detector says.

</Callout>

## Write somewhere that keeps history

Google Docs and Word (saved to OneDrive) both log every edit with timestamps. A document built up over several sessions looks nothing like one pasted in whole, and that history is the single most convincing thing you can show if you’re ever asked. Writing in a plain text editor with no autosave history throws this away for free — worth avoiding for anything graded.

## Keep the scaffolding

Outlines, notes, source lists, and any messages with a tutor or study group about the assignment. None of this is required reading for anyone unless a question comes up — but if one does, it answers “how did you write this” far better than a percentage ever could.

## Save your own report before you submit

Run your finished draft through Litimus yourself and keep the result. If your instructor’s tool flags something later, you’ll already know whether it’s consistent with what you saw, and you’ll have a timestamp from before the assignment was due.

## What a low score doesn’t mean

A clean report isn’t proof either — it’s just one more data point. Formulaic sections, lab report formats, and short structured answers can score oddly in both directions. The trail above matters more than the number either way.
