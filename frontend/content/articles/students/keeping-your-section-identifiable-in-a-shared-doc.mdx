## Write your section, don’t just edit into one

If you draft your contribution in your own pass rather than typing directly inside a teammate’s existing paragraphs, Google Docs and Word version history can attribute it to you specifically. Constant back-and-forth editing inside the same paragraph makes that much harder to untangle later.

## Keep a note of who did what, separate from the document itself

A shared outline or task list assigning sections by name is useful evidence on its own, independent of what the document’s edit history shows — and easier for an instructor to read quickly than a full revision log.

## If the whole document gets flagged

A single high score on a combined document doesn’t tell anyone which section produced it. If this happens, be ready to point to your specific contribution and its own history — see <a href="/for/teachers/group-projects-and-ai-detection-whose-score-is-it">how an instructor is likely to approach this</a> for what they should be doing on their end.

## Raise unclear group rules before they matter

If your group hasn’t agreed on what AI assistance is acceptable for a shared document, that is worth settling with your teammates before you submit, not after someone’s section gets questioned.
