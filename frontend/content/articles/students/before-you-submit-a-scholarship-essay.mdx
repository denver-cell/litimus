## Check it yourself before a platform does

Run your finished essay through Litimus before you submit, and read the reasons, not just the number. If something reads as templated — a common risk in personal-statement essays that lean on familiar structures — you have time to revise it while it still matters.

## Keep your drafts even after you submit

Scholarship platforms rarely offer an appeals conversation the way a class assignment might. If a decision is ever questioned, your own draft history is the only record you will have, since no one on the other end saw you write it.

## A personal statement has its own risk profile

Common essay structures and reflective language can read as more uniform than an argumentative essay does, simply because the genre encourages a certain shape. That is a known limitation of statistical detection, not a reason to avoid writing a personal statement the normal way.

## If the platform’s checker flags something you can’t contest

Some platforms make automated decisions with no review step. If that is genuinely a risk for a scholarship that matters to you, checking and revising beforehand is the only real safeguard you have.
