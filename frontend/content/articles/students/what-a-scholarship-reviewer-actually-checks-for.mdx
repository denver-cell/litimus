## Reviewers read volume, and it shows in what they notice

Someone reading two hundred essays on the same prompt develops a fast sense of which ones sound like every other one — the same opening line, the same arc, the same safe conclusion. That instinct exists independent of any AI-detection tool and predates all of them.

## Specificity is what both a reviewer and a detector reward

A detail only you could know — a specific moment, a specific person, a specific reason — is what makes an essay memorable to a reader and what tends to keep a detector’s score lower too. These are not two different things to optimize for; they are the same thing.

## Don’t write to the average of the prompt

The version of your essay that answers the prompt the way most applicants will is the version most likely to blend in, to a reviewer and a detector alike. Write the version only you could have written.

## Checking your own draft is still worth doing

Not because a reviewer necessarily runs one, but because reading the reasons behind a mid-range score is a useful, specific edit note — see <a href="/for/students/before-you-submit-a-scholarship-essay">checking before you submit</a> for how to use that report well.
