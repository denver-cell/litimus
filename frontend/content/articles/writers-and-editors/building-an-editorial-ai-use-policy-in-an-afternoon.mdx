## Four decisions, not forty

1. What’s allowed without disclosure — research, brainstorming, fact-checking.
2. What requires disclosure — drafting assistance, editing passes.
3. What isn’t allowed at all, if anything.
4. What happens if a piece is flagged after submission — a conversation first, per <a href="/for/writers-and-editors/second-opinion-not-a-verdict-using-a-detector-in-your-edit-pass">using a detector as a second opinion</a>, not an automatic rejection.

## Put it in the submission guidelines, not a separate document

Contributors read submission guidelines. They don’t read a linked policy page they have to click through to find. Say the actual rule where the actual deadline is.

## Say what a score does and doesn’t mean, if you check

If you run submissions through a detector, tell contributors that a mid-range score isn’t a rejection on its own — see <a href="/for/writers-and-editors">the six signals in editorial terms</a> for language you can borrow. Contributors who understand the process file fewer disputes than contributors who find out about it after being flagged.

## Revisit it in six months

This is a fast-moving area; a policy written today will need a look again once tools and norms shift. Put a date on it and actually check back.
