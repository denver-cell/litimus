## Why a good edit can raise a score

Evening out sentence length, cutting stray tangents, and standardizing terminology — all normal, good editing — reduce exactly the variation a detector reads as human. A well-edited piece can end up statistically closer to generated text than the rougher draft it started as.

## This is a real limitation, not a loophole to exploit

It means a score is not a reliable signal on heavily edited work either direction — not because editing is suspicious, but because the detector cannot distinguish a good edit from generation using rhythm alone. That is worth knowing, not worth hiding from a client who asks.

## What to check before you assume the writer used a tool

Ask for the pre-edit draft. A rougher, more uneven original that you smoothed into a tidier final version is a completely ordinary editorial process, and the draft itself is the evidence, not the final score.

## Say this to a client upfront

If you do heavy structural edits as part of your process, telling a client in advance that this can affect a detector score if they ever run one avoids a confusing conversation after the fact.
