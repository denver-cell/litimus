## Three versions of the same rule

Some guidelines mean no tool involvement at any stage, including brainstorming. Others mean specifically no generated prose in the submitted draft, with research or editing tools left unaddressed. A few mean only that undisclosed use will disqualify a submission — disclosed use is fine. These are meaningfully different rules using the same three words.

## When the wording is genuinely unclear

Email the editor and ask, rather than guess at the strictest or most permissive reading. A specific question about your specific situation gets you a real answer faster than trying to infer one from a one-line policy.

## Why publications are still figuring this out themselves

Most outlets did not have to define this two years ago, and many are writing policy reactively rather than from a settled position. A guideline that reads as vague may simply be new — not evasive.

## What to do if your work gets questioned anyway

Keep the same evidence any writer should keep: drafts, notes, research trail. See <a href="/for/writers-and-editors/submission-guidelines-are-starting-to-mention-ai-detectors">the broader disclosure trend</a> for what that evidence is generally worth once a guideline like this is in place.
