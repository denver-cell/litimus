## Run it after your first pass, not instead of one

A detector is cheapest to use as a second read on a draft you’ve already edited once — by then you have your own sense of the piece, and the report either confirms it or points somewhere you missed.

## The two signals worth reading regardless of score

**Stock transitions.** “Moreover,” “it is important to note,” “in conclusion” — connective filler you’d cut in any edit, whoever wrote it. If the report flags these, that’s a tightening note, not an accusation.

**Repeated three-word phrases.** The same short phrase turning up more than once in a piece is a copy-edit flag independent of where it came from. Worth fixing either way.

## Where the score itself is less useful

Heavily edited AI output lands in the middle of the range or lower — a borderline score tells you little on its own. And formulaic house styles (press releases, structured reviews, templated product copy) can read as templated to any statistical detector without a word of AI involvement. Treat a mid-range score as “worth a closer read,” not as a finding.

## Write the policy down before you need it

Decide what counts as acceptable AI use for your publication before a submission forces the question, and tell contributors. A score is only useful measured against a policy someone agreed to.
