## Volume work rewards exactly what a detector flags

A content brief written to a strict word count, keyword list and heading structure, on a tight per-piece rate, pushes toward the same formulaic patterns a detector measures — consistent structure, safe phrasing, predictable transitions. That is often the brief working as intended, not a shortcut.

## Bylined feature writing has more room, and more scrutiny

A feature piece with a distinct voice and looser structure has more room to read as clearly human — and readers and editors alike tend to expect that distinctiveness, which makes a mid-range score more worth investigating here than in volume content.

## Know which genre you’re being read as before you argue the score

If a client applies feature-writing expectations to an SEO brief, or vice versa, that mismatch is worth naming directly rather than accepting the wrong comparison. The same six signals mean something different depending on what the piece was actually commissioned to be.

## Where this doesn’t excuse anything

Genre tolerance for formula is not the same as a blanket exemption from a client’s actual AI-use policy. A formulaic brief can still be entirely human-written and still need to follow whatever disclosure rule applies.
